# Flood Safety: Citizen Emergency Guidance

> **Source and use note:** This is a curated, paraphrased summary of the
> WHO EMRO flood guidance for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), not
> a verbatim copy of the webpage and not a substitute for official local
> instructions or professional medical care. The source is written for
> the WHO Eastern Mediterranean Region; local details must be checked
> for the Philippines.
>
> **Primary source:** [WHO EMRO --- Flood preparedness and response
> guidance](https://www.emro.who.int/rcce/floods.html)

## 1. Immediate priorities

-   Protect life first. Floodwater can be deep, fast-moving,
    contaminated, and concealing holes, debris, or other hazards.
-   Follow current official weather alerts, evacuation orders, and
    instructions from local authorities and emergency responders.
-   If an evacuation order is issued, leave using the recommended safe
    route. Do not delay to save belongings.
-   Do not walk, wade, swim, or drive through floodwater. Do not bypass
    road barricades.
-   If you cannot safely leave, move to higher ground or an upper floor
    if safe to do so, and contact emergency services. If stranded in a
    safe elevated location, remain there and signal for help.
-   Keep away from downed power lines, wet electrical equipment,
    unstable buildings, and floodwater touching electrical wires.
-   Seek medical help for injuries, illness, animal bites, or concerning
    symptoms after a flood.

## 2. Before a flood: prepare and stay informed

### Monitor warnings

-   Follow official weather reports, radio broadcasts, and local
    government or emergency-service announcements.
-   A flood watch generally means flooding is possible; a flood warning
    means flooding is occurring or expected soon. The exact terminology
    can vary by country, so follow the definitions used by local
    authorities.
-   Treat continuous or intense rain as a reason to monitor official
    alerts and local conditions closely. Do not wait until water enters
    the home if authorities advise evacuation or the area is becoming
    unsafe.
-   Learn whether your home, route to work or school, and nearby roads
    are in flood-prone areas. Ask local officials or neighbours about
    local flood patterns and safer routes.

### Prepare household essentials

-   Keep important documents in a waterproof bag.
-   Prepare a go-bag with essential medicines, clothes, hygiene items, a
    flashlight, batteries, a radio if available, phone charger or power
    bank, cash, and emergency contact details.
-   Keep enough safe drinking water and shelf-stable food for several
    days if possible.
-   Have a first-aid kit and essential prescription medicines available.
-   Charge mobile phones and power banks before severe weather if
    possible.
-   Plan how children, older adults, people with disabilities, pregnant
    people, people with chronic conditions, and pets will evacuate.
-   Agree on a household meeting point and a way to contact one another
    if separated.

### Protect the home, only when it is safe

-   Move valuables, documents, medicines, and hazardous materials above
    expected flood levels.
-   If flooding is likely and it is safe to do so, switch off
    electricity and utilities according to local utility guidance. Do
    not touch electrical switches or equipment while standing in water
    or when they are wet.
-   Do not put yourself at risk trying to secure property. Personal
    safety takes priority over belongings.

## 3. During a flood: rising water and evacuation

### If floodwater is approaching

1.  Check official warnings and follow evacuation instructions
    immediately.
2.  Leave early if ordered to evacuate or if your location is becoming
    unsafe. Do not wait for water to reach the house.
3.  Use a recommended route to higher ground or an official evacuation
    site. Avoid rivers, streams, drains, underpasses, and flooded roads.
4.  Do not walk or wade through floodwater. Water can be deeper or
    faster than it looks and may contain debris, open drains, sharp
    objects, sewage, chemicals, or electrical hazards.
5.  If you cannot safely evacuate, move to a higher floor or higher
    ground if accessible without entering floodwater. Contact emergency
    services and communicate your location.
6.  Stay away from buildings that appear damaged or unstable.
7.  Do not return to a flooded area until local authorities or
    authorized responders say it is safe.

### If you are in a vehicle

-   Never drive through flowing water or a flooded road or bridge. Turn
    around and choose another route.
-   Do not drive around barricades. Avoid underpasses and avoid
    unnecessary night travel during floods.
-   If your vehicle stalls in rising water, prioritize getting out and
    moving to higher ground if you can do so safely.
-   If a vehicle is becoming submerged, escape as quickly as possible if
    an exit is available; do not remain in the vehicle to protect
    belongings.
-   Do not enter floodwater to attempt a vehicle rescue. Call emergency
    services.

### If you are trapped or stranded

-   Move to a safe elevated location if you can do so without entering
    dangerous water.
-   Contact local emergency services and provide your location, the
    number of people with you, any injuries, and any urgent
    accessibility or medical needs.
-   Make yourself visible or signal for help when safe.
-   Do not attempt to swim through floodwater or cross a fast-moving
    current.

## 4. Prolonged rain and uncertainty about whether to evacuate

-   Keep monitoring official meteorological bulletins and local
    government announcements while heavy rain continues.
-   Do not use the amount of rain alone to decide that it is safe to
    stay. Local drainage, river levels, terrain, upstream rainfall, and
    sudden flash flooding can change risk quickly.
-   Know the local evacuation route and the nearest official evacuation
    location before conditions deteriorate.
-   Follow evacuation orders promptly. If the home is threatened or you
    feel unsafe, seek official advice and move to safety if you can do
    so safely.
-   Do not wait for the AI assistant to confirm a hazard. Use current
    official alerts and emergency-service instructions for
    time-sensitive decisions.

## 5. After a flood: returning and entering buildings

-   Wait for the official all-clear before returning home or entering
    affected areas.
-   Avoid standing water and places where water is still collecting.
-   Keep children away from floodwater and recently flooded areas.
-   Do not touch wet electrical equipment, downed wires, or water that
    may be in contact with electrical lines.
-   Treat damaged buildings as potentially unsafe. Do not enter
    structures that appear unstable.
-   Have a qualified professional check electrical systems and gas
    appliances before using them again.
-   If using a generator to power equipment or dry a building, operate
    it outdoors and away from doors, windows, and vents. Generators
    produce carbon monoxide, a deadly gas that cannot be seen or
    smelled.
-   Seek medical attention if you are injured or ill, need essential
    prescription refills, develop flu-like symptoms, or have been bitten
    by an animal or insect.
-   Seek emotional or mental-health support if stress, anxiety,
    depression, or feeling overwhelmed persists.

## 6. Floodwater exposure and illness prevention

-   Avoid direct contact with floodwater whenever possible. It may
    contain sewage, harmful microorganisms, chemicals, sharp debris, and
    other contaminants.
-   Keep cuts and open wounds out of floodwater. If a wound is exposed,
    wash it with clean water and soap as soon as possible, cover it with
    a clean dressing, and seek medical advice for deep wounds,
    punctures, contaminated wounds, or signs of infection.
-   Wash exposed skin with soap and clean water as soon as possible.
    Remove contaminated clothing and clean it safely.
-   Seek medical advice after potentially contaminated floodwater
    exposure if you have a wound, are concerned about exposure, or
    develop fever or other illness.
-   In the Philippines, ask local health authorities about leptospirosis
    risks and current guidance after exposure to floodwater. Do not rely
    on this general document for diagnosis or medication decisions.
-   Watch for symptoms after exposure and seek prompt clinical advice if
    you become ill. Do not wait for severe symptoms before contacting a
    health professional.

**Medical safety note:** This WHO flood overview is not a complete
first-aid or clinical protocol. Pair it with an authoritative wound-care
source and current Philippine Department of Health guidance. The AI
should not diagnose leptospirosis or recommend prescription antibiotics
on its own.

## 7. Safe drinking water

-   Drink water from a safe, reliable source or water treated using
    methods recommended by local health authorities.
-   If authorities advise boiling, bring water to a rolling boil for the
    locally recommended duration, then let it cool and store it in a
    clean, covered container. Follow local guidance where fuel,
    altitude, or water contamination conditions affect the advice.
-   If using chlorine or another water-treatment product, follow
    official instructions for the specific product and water conditions.
    Do not guess a dose.
-   Store drinking water in clean, covered containers that prevent hands
    and contaminants from entering.
-   Do not assume tap water is safe after flooding. Follow announcements
    from the local water provider and health authorities.

## 8. Food safety after flooding

-   Discard food that has touched floodwater, food in packaging that
    cannot be cleaned safely, and food with unusual smell or appearance.
-   Do not eat food if you are unsure whether it has been contaminated
    or kept at a safe temperature.
-   Use safe water to wash hands, prepare food, and clean utensils.
-   Wash hands with soap and clean water before preparing food and
    eating, and after using the toilet.
-   Cook food thoroughly and eat it promptly. Protect food from flies
    and other contaminants.
-   Follow local public-health instructions for disinfecting intact,
    sealed food containers. Do not apply a bleach recipe from a
    different context without verifying the concentration and
    instructions with a trusted local health source.

## 9. Cleaning and drying the home

-   Do not begin cleanup until authorities say the area is safe and
    electrical hazards have been addressed.
-   Wear appropriate protective equipment for the hazards present; avoid
    direct contact with floodwater, sewage, sharp debris, and
    contaminated materials.
-   Move wet items outside to dry when it is safe. Remove standing water
    only when doing so does not expose people to electrical, structural,
    or contamination hazards.
-   Ventilate the home when weather and security conditions allow.
-   Clean and disinfect flood-affected surfaces and items using product
    labels and public-health guidance. Work in a well-ventilated area
    and never mix cleaning chemicals.
-   Remove or safely remediate mold. People with asthma, respiratory
    conditions, immune compromise, or other vulnerabilities should avoid
    hazardous cleanup and seek appropriate assistance.
-   Do not operate fuel-burning equipment indoors or in enclosed spaces
    because carbon monoxide can build up and cause death.

## 10. Insects, rodents, and other hazards

-   Flooding can leave standing water that supports mosquito breeding.
    Follow local health advice on removing standing water where safe,
    using screens or mosquito nets, and using approved repellents.
-   Floods may increase contact with rats and other pests. Avoid contact
    with animal urine, faeces, carcasses, and contaminated materials.
-   Seek medical advice after animal bites or scratches, and follow
    local guidance for wound care and vaccination assessment.
-   Be alert to damaged roads, bridges, riverbanks, buildings, exposed
    wires, gas leaks, and chemical spills.
-   Do not enter areas with suspected chemical contamination. Follow
    local authorities' instructions.

## 11. Supporting people who may need extra assistance

-   Plan evacuation and communication for children, older adults,
    pregnant people, people with disabilities, and people with chronic
    physical or mental-health conditions.
-   Keep children close to a parent or trusted caregiver. Reassure them,
    listen to their concerns, and maintain familiar routines where
    possible.
-   Check on neighbours who may have difficulty receiving warnings,
    evacuating, or accessing medicines and care, but do not put yourself
    in danger.
-   Protect privacy and dignity when helping displaced people. Use
    trusted services to report suspected abuse or exploitation.
-   People who rely on regular medication, prenatal or postnatal care,
    diabetes management, or other ongoing treatment should seek help to
    maintain that care after the flood.

### Suggested answer policy for the AI

1.  Give immediate life-safety actions first for imminent danger.
2.  Never advise users to walk, wade, or drive through floodwater.
3.  Defer to current official warnings and evacuation orders over static
    documents.
4.  State when local or live information is required; do not invent
    current conditions, phone numbers, shelters, or evacuation orders.
5.  For serious bleeding, breathing difficulty, severe injury, or acute
    illness, direct the user to emergency services or urgent medical
    care.
6.  Keep clinical advice within the scope of reviewed first-aid sources.
    Do not diagnose or prescribe medication.
7.  Cite the source document and relevant section in generated answers.
8.  Keep this WHO source labelled as international guidance and
    supplement it with Philippine government sources.
